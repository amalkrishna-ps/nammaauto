Vehicle geometry, street scenery, rear route emblems and sounds are original
procedural work. The emblem is text-free decoration with no human likeness and
remains the default API. The player and all four traffic autos explicitly select
their separate local image sets.

Enhanced 3D vehicles, people, facades, pavement and drains are original procedural
work; surface grain, road wear and mud texture are generated locally, not imported
surface images. TVR cinema's 17.5m pylon, front-lit TVR CINEMAS sign, marquee and
ticket counter are original geometry/sign artwork. Its five original posters use
CGF - Colar Gold Fields, with the full title painted COLAR GOLD FIELDS (Colar, not
Kolar). The existing Bollywood-inspired fictional faces and composition remain:
no external poster images, actor photographs or real-film reproductions are used.
These original posters are separate from the user-supplied third-party photograph
described below.

Selective inward bevels on vehicles, architecture and actor details, and smoother
organic curves on people, cows and dogs, are project-authored procedural geometry.
Authored silhouettes, outer bounds and collision footprints are preserved. Broad
faces retain flat normals rather than pinched shading; curves retain analytic or
correctly transformed normals. Geometry checks cover finite unit normals and
nondegenerate refined triangles, with collapsed vehicle rounded-box strips removed
without replacing authored normals.

Physically based actor surfaces use three shared generated 1024 x 512 atlases:
sRGB tint, linear packed height/roughness/metalness, and linear clearcoat-mask/
clearcoat-roughness data. Opaque vehicle batches use MeshPhysicalMaterial with
clearcoat masked to paint ONLY. Bare metal, rubber/tyre sidewalls, cloth, leather,
skin and hair are not clearcoated, including riders sharing the vehicle batch.
Only exposed metal is metallic. Standalone road actors and player drivers remain
MeshStandardMaterial, using the tint and physical maps without clearcoat.
Multiscale grain, directional metal machining, rubber pores, woven cloth, leather
creases and fine skin pores are original procedural synthesis. Five explicit
tile-local mip levels downsample tint in linear light, with edge-extruded gutters
keeping material classes separate.

World surfaces use four cached 512 x 512 maps per world: color and packed
height/roughness pairs for the plaster/concrete/paving atlas and asphalt. Coarse
angular aggregate and subtle tar-repair patches refine asphalt; concrete has
pores, spalling and rain-streak stains. Manual mip chains area-average color in
linear light and keep packed data linear. Wrapped gutters and per-level atlas
sampling prevent neighboring material bleed. Vehicle and world glass use
nonmetallic MeshPhysicalMaterial with IOR 1.5 and no transmission. These material
refinements retain existing batches without extra geometry or draw calls; bump
detail complements geometry normals. No imported models, scans, surface images
or normal maps are added. The aim is more realistic procedural rendering, not
photorealism. These surface-only refinements do not themselves alter physics
or gameplay.

Asphalt uses world-space XZ sampling with smooth domain warping and correlated
color/height/roughness variation to break up obvious tile repeats. The 4m tile,
existing maps and five texture reads remain; added shader arithmetic requires no
new textures, geometry or draw calls. Thirty project-authored seeded tree crowns
vary height, aspect, lean, orientation, branches and leaf-card silhouettes, with
varied alpha-tested shadows from the same baked geometry. Crowns stay stable
across frames, resets and recycling; the existing 500m pool still repeats. Trunks,
tree-island blockers and street layout remain unchanged. Crown variation adds no
textures, materials, draw calls, vertices or per-frame resources. No foliage
images are imported.

The outdoor sky/ground gradient and broad sun are original procedural artwork,
not a downloaded HDR or third-party room scene. Three.js PMREM filters this
environment once per lighting instance. The neutral texture is reused while
simulation-time weather adjusts its intensity, visible sky, sun, hemisphere fill
and fog. Physically based daylight uses stabilized PCF shadows: 2048 x 2048 HIGH,
1024 x 1024 BALANCED, off LOW; output uses ACES filmic tone mapping, exposure 0.95
and sRGB. The project-authored camera cycles C: LOW CHASE -> ELEVATED -> COCKPIT.
New runs and menu return reset the driving-camera selection to LOW CHASE,
restoring the original settled eye 4.9m above ground and 9.5m behind the auto,
targeting 1.05m above ground and 11m ahead (about 10.64-degree elevation).
ELEVATED is 45 degrees with 10m look-ahead. Both exterior views follow auto heading
with shortest-angle interpolation while staying upright through rollover.
The home view is unchanged: 45-degree-azimuth front-quarter at 18-degree elevation.
Exterior transitions are smooth; entering/leaving cockpit snaps. Motion pauses.

The cockpit is original physical 3D geometry: two hands wrap the turning handlebar,
with Anna's khaki or Akka's teal sleeves and rolled cuffs. Detailed PBR grips,
levers/cables, gauges, a speed-driven needle, switches and key use existing actor
surface maps. The open windshield has no glass plane obscuring the actual street.
The eye follows actual auto yaw, suspension and tumble, not a screen overlay;
exterior color/depth masking preserves the auto shadow. Geometry and both sleeve
variants are cached; no new cockpit texture or external image is introduced.

wrong-way bikes. The original unbranded electric twist-throttle step-through
mopeds have integrated batteries, rear hub motors, black seats, brake levers/cables
and both feet on fixed footrests. There are no pedals, chains, pedalling or Yulu
logos. Bag colors/labels remain unchanged: four Tomato, three Krypto and three
yellow/green ThinkIt bags. No imported vehicle model or new external image is used.
